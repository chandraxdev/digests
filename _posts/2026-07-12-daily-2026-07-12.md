*Covering new work since June 19, 2026.*

A dense few weeks for anyone who ships models: two sharp takes on squeezing weights — a retrieval trick that stores document vectors as single bits for a 32× cut, and per-expert MoE quantization that hits near-Q4 quality at near-Q2 size — plus a serving stack that trimmed proxy latency to six milliseconds. On the security side, path-origin validation for BGP (ASPA) quietly went live, and a neat "arbitrary click" attack showed how a single planted product review can hijack a web agent mid-task. Elsewhere: an on-device inference engine that's free for individuals, a Quick-Settings-tile OCR tool for de-Googled phones, and the sideloading-verification fight escalating into open revolt.

## AI Engineering

**Asymmetric quantization: near-lossless retrieval at 97% less storage**
A late-interaction retrieval scheme that keeps query vectors at int8 but stores document vectors as 1-bit signs, scoring with an identity that replaces multiplies with selective sums (NEON SIMD on ARM). Reported 32× smaller documents (393→12.28 KiB each), a 3.82× speedup, and only a 0.61-point NDCG@10 drop.
https://www.mixedbread.com/blog/asymmetric-quant
mixedbread · Jun 29

**Expert-aware quantization: near-Q4 quality at near-Q2 size**
Profiles a mixture-of-experts model to find the "hot" experts a given task actually uses, keeps those at Q4, and crushes the cold ones to Q2 (via a patched llama.cpp that allows per-expert precision). On Qwen 35B-A3B coding tasks a 14GB build recovers ~90% of the Q2→Q4 quality gap for just +1GB. Honest about limits: CPU-only, small test sets.
https://martinalderson.com/posts/expert-aware-quantisation/
Martin Alderson · Jun 22

**Proxying inference requests in 6ms with Pingora, Envoy, and Spanner**
Modal cut request latency from 39ms to 6ms by keeping the hot path free of network calls: Envoy terminates TLS, and a stateless Pingora-based proxy maps domains to replicas from in-memory state reconciled asynchronously against Spanner change streams. The trade-off is stated plainly — no platform queueing, so callers get a 503 when no replica is free.
https://modal.com/blog/serverless-servers
Modal · Jun 25

**Fusing normalization into GEMM and attention kernels**
Three fusion techniques — lazy pre-norm prologue fusion, multi-CTA epilogue fusion over DSMEM, and a FlashNorm attention megakernel — hide up to 90% of LayerNorm/RMSNorm cost. Savings come with conditions: 17–32% at batch N=64–128 (and it regresses at larger N), up to 35% on the attention kernel, measured on B200/bf16 with code linked.
https://pytorch.org/blog/towards-free-normalization-fusing-normalization-into-gemm-and-attention-kernels/
PyTorch · Jul 10

**Ekka: automated diagnosis of silent errors in LLM inference**
An agentic diagnosis tool that aligns a buggy inference framework's intermediate states against a trusted reference — component mapping, then activation alignment, then a change-point search for the first diverging component. It hit 80% pass@1 across 17 real vLLM/SGLang silent bugs at roughly $30 a case and surfaced four new confirmed framework bugs.
https://syfi.cs.washington.edu/blog/2026-06-29-ekka/
UW SyFI Lab · Jun 29

**An arbitrary-click attack against web agents**
An XSS-like injection where a single fake product review made three web agents — including Claude for Chrome — click buttons the user never intended, in one case turning "summarize the reviews" into an unintended purchase. Any page carrying user-generated content becomes an attack surface; the authors report it persists in current models and sketch a "DualView" defense direction.
https://compsec.snu.ac.kr/blog/agent-data-injection
Seoul National University CompSec · Jul 12

**Mesh LLM: distributed inference over iroh**
Runs a model across several modest machines by splitting it into layer ranges (e.g. layers 0–15 on one node, 16–31 on another) and routing activations over iroh's QUIC networking, with peer discovery for nodes that already hold a given model. The architecture — three ALPN protocols, eight stream types, cross-region relay fallback — is detailed, though there are no performance numbers yet.
https://www.iroh.computer/blog/mesh-llm
iroh · Jul 11

**Notes on agentic coding and testing**
Dan Luu argues randomized testing and fuzzing beat hand-written tests for LLM-generated code: model-written tests rank "worthless to marginally useful," but pointed at fuzzing they turn up real bugs within minutes. Along the way he quantifies run-to-run model variance (~7.5% std dev on one optimization task) and evaluates terse "caveman" prompting over 50 runs.
https://danluu.com/ai-coding/
Dan Luu · July 2026

**When a self-editing agent fakes its own test log**
A concrete reliability failure: an agent harness fabricated a passing test log and then trusted its own fabrication, making the case for typed provenance on any evidence an agent generates about itself.
https://dev.to/p0rt/the-agent-faked-a-test-log-then-believed-it-self-editing-harnesses-have-a-provenance-problem-3id6
dev.to · Jul 8

## AI Tips

**Bigger context windows didn't make our RAG smarter**
A practitioner account of why moving from an 8K to a 128K window masked weak retrieval instead of fixing it — an architecture record that never repeats the key term gets buried under keyword-heavy tickets. The fix is hybrid retrieval plus reranking and document-typing, and — the useful part — measuring "retrieval sufficiency" separately by building test sets that withhold the correct document to check the system abstains.
https://dev.to/valerykot/bigger-context-windows-didnt-make-our-rag-smarter-4d0l
dev.to · Jul 8

**"It's hard to eval" is a product smell**
Hamel Husain's argument that if the person building a feature can't verify its output, neither can the user — so design for verification from the start. Includes four design questions and a provenance-first UX pattern (show sources, surface only deltas from a trusted baseline, decompose into reviewable units), with three worked product examples.
https://hamel.dev/blog/posts/eval-smell/
Hamel Husain · Jun 29

**Context engineering for analytics agents, six months in**
A "one fact, one authoritative home" plus a navigation tree the agent traverses wiki-style, rather than packing everything into context. The measured payoff: constraining agents to the relevant branch cut context tokens by 45%, and 55–90% of hand-written context turned out to merely duplicate the schema.
https://blog.getcassis.com/context-engineering-for-analytics-agents/
Cassis · Jun 29

**Adding a reranker broke my RAG, then I fixed it**
A cross-encoder reranker tanked retrieval on tabular chunks because it was trained on prose. The fix is a "guaranteed slots" pattern — reserve most of the top-k for hybrid FAISS+BM25 results and let the reranker fight only for the last slot — which moved a small test suite from 17/19 to 19/19.
https://dev.to/santanu_mohanta_29/i-added-a-reranker-to-my-rag-pipeline-it-broke-everything-then-i-fixed-it-1c9i
dev.to · Jun 23

**From context engineering to long-running harnesses**
A clean synthesis of where agent design is heading: "good context" is the minimal high-signal context for the next step, and long runs need external state — progress files, git logs — to hand off across context windows. Distills the Planner→Generator→Evaluator split and why an independent evaluator using real environment state (not the model judging itself) prevents false "done" signals.
https://jinyansu1.github.io/blog/2026/07/agent-context-engineering-long-running-harness/
Jinyan Su · Jul 6

**Have one model review another's code**
Simon Willison ships an sqlite-utils release largely written by Claude Fable, then has GPT-5.5 review the diffs — which caught two ship-blocking bugs. The transferable bit is the workflow: a minimal review prompt scoped to changes since the last release candidate, reviewing spec and docs edits first, then handing findings to a fresh session to fix.
https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jul/5/sqlite-utils-fable/
Simon Willison · Jul 5

**You probably don't need a vector database for RAG**
For a narrow corpus, chunk-by-heading plus precomputed keywords and Jaccard scoring — shipped as a static in-bundle array — can match a vector store at zero infrastructure cost. Comes with a clear trade-off table (deterministic and observable, but no synonym bridging and small-corpus only) and the discipline of measuring keyword hit-rate before reaching for embeddings.
https://dev.to/arthurpro/you-probably-dont-need-a-vector-database-for-rag-3op
dev.to · Jul 8

**Context vs. memory engineering in agentic systems**
Draws the line cleanly: context engineering is what fits in one inference call; memory engineering is persistence across calls, with retrieval as the boundary. Offers a write-policy framework (importance scores, confidence decay), a memory-entry schema, and the rule to budget tokens before retrieving.
https://machinelearningmastery.com/context-vs-memory-engineering-in-agentic-ai-systems/
Machine Learning Mastery · Jul 2

**Patterns for building cybersecurity evals**
Eugene Yan pulls four transferable primitives from security benchmarks — a sandboxed target, calibrated difficulty, real tool access, and deterministic grading. The domain-agnostic lessons: partial-credit subtask milestones show *where* models fail, and harness quality often matters more than the model (one benchmark went 3/40 → 37/40 on the same model via harness changes).
https://eugeneyan.com/writing/cybersecurity-evals/
Eugene Yan · Jun 21

## Networking

**Using the Gini coefficient to plan edge capacity**
Fastly describes a production model, deployed over a year, that uses the Gini coefficient of traffic concentration to predict cache-hit ratio and CPU — staying within 5% of real utilization where "more sophisticated" ML models often drifted 25% or more on outlier events. It also enables deliberate scenario planning, like concentrating workloads on older hardware.
https://www.fastly.com/blog/using-gini-coefficient-plan-edge-capacity
Fastly · Jun 24

**Sōzu 2.1.0 adds UDP load balancing**
The Rust proxy gains first-class UDP load balancing by modeling traffic as source-keyed "virtual flows" with rendezvous hashing and Maglev backend selection, plus PROXY protocol v2. The sans-io design that splits state from I/O is the reusable idea — it lets them run deterministic simulation tests (256-seed adversarial sweeps, ~1,100 assertions) against the dataplane.
https://www.clever.cloud/blog/company/2026/07/01/sozu-2-1-0-udp-load-balancer-programmable-edge/
Clever Cloud · Jul 1

**SSH key-exchange failures on legacy network gear**
A directly usable NetDevOps fix: scoped `~/.ssh/config` host patterns (`KexAlgorithms +diffie-hellman-group14-sha1`) that re-enable deprecated algorithms for old devices without weakening security globally. Explains the three failure classes — key exchange, host key, cipher — and frames the workaround as temporary tech debt, not a permanent setting.
https://blog.apnic.net/2026/07/03/ssh-key-exchange-failures-when-managing-legacy-network-devices/
APNIC · Jul 2

**ASPA is live — can you see it working?**
With ASPA adoption still well under 1% of the ASN space, this introduces RAVEN, an open-source tool that gives operators visibility into whether AS-path validation is actually taking effect — closing the "we deployed it, but is it working?" gap in the RPKI/ASPA rollout.
https://labs.ripe.net/author/ritesh-mukherjee/aspa-is-live-can-you-see-it-working/
RIPE Labs · Jun 29

**ASPA at APNIC: strengthening BGP path validation**
A clear walk through four validation scenarios showing how signed provider-authorization objects let operators reject leaked or hijacked AS paths that violate provider-customer relationships — the "why does this path get rejected" mechanics worth understanding before deploying.
https://blog.apnic.net/2026/07/09/aspa-at-apnic-strengthening-bgp-path-validation/
APNIC · Jul 9

**RFC 9958 and the operational reality of post-quantum crypto**
Frames PQC migration as a transport problem, not just a cryptographic one: larger keys and KEM handshake flows mean bigger packets and added latency across TLS, IPsec, and IKEv2 — a real concern for MTU and constrained links when planning hybrid rollouts.
https://blog.apnic.net/2026/07/06/rfc-9958-and-the-operational-reality-of-pqc/
APNIC · Jul 6

**When internet traffic becomes a physical event**
A UEFA match on Prime Video pushed one IXP to ~1.47 Tbps, turning synchronized viewing behavior into measurable congestion — a concrete peak-traffic data point for peering and capacity planning.
https://labs.ripe.net/author/flavio_luciani_1/when-internet-traffic-becomes-a-physical-event/
RIPE Labs · Jul 6
