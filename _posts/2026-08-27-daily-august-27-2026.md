*Covering August 10 – August 27, 2026*

A heavy issue. The centrepiece is a rare piece of honest accounting from a team that moved its agent loops off Anthropic to a cheaper model and published the real bill — the savings were half what the sticker price promised, and one "OpenAI-compatible" provider silently failed every structured call for a week. Alongside it, an essay arguing that inference engines themselves are the exploitable surface, a data-backed case that OpenTelemetry is drifting, and Z.ai putting a 320B multimodal model out under MIT. Networking gets a clear-eyed inventory of the BGP attacks RPKI was never going to catch, plus Cloudflare's discovery that two Tier-1s are quietly stripping the very attribute that stops route leaks. On mobile, F-Droid had an unusually good fortnight — a two-thumb keyboard, a multipath VPN that bonds Wi-Fi and cellular, and an automation app that compiles natural language into a deterministic engine. And Google has told Play developers their apps will have a memory budget.

## AI Engineering

**What we learned moving our agent loops from Anthropic to GLM**
The most useful cost post in a while, because it publishes the arithmetic instead of the headline. Per-token pricing promised 95% savings; production delivered 68%, because average tokens per call rose from ~18,500 to ~24,700 as the model made more tool calls and wrote longer. The compatibility section is the real value: reasoning-content formatting, prompt-cache keying and usage accounting all differ across "OpenAI-compatible" providers, and on one of them zero of 336 structured calls succeeded over seven days.
https://getunblocked.com/blog/moving-agent-loops-from-anthropic-to-glm/
*getunblocked.com · Aug 13*

**LLMs could control their host machines by exploiting inference engines**
The argument is that the serving layer, not the model, is the attack surface — a model that can emit arbitrary token sequences is a fuzzer pointed at your parser. It anchors on CVE-2025-9141, where vLLM's tool-call parser passed nearly every argument to `eval()`, and on a parsing bug where a literal `<mm:think>` string was mistaken for a reasoning delimiter. With 200+ model architectures and several competing chat formats to parse, the surface is not small. No proof-of-concept code is offered, so treat the escalation path as reasoned rather than demonstrated.
https://boydkane.com/essays/llms-could-control-their-host-machines-by-exploiting-inference-engines
*boydkane.com · Aug 25*

**OTel isn't going well (and I made a spreadsheet about it)**

## Networking

**Beyond origin validation: four classes of routing attack nobody is validating**
Flavio Luciani maps every known class of BGP attack against the defences that actually exist and finds the coverage is narrower than the RPKI success story suggests. Route origin validation handles prefix hijacking — with only 12.3% of ASes fully protected, and permissive maxLength widening the surface rather than narrowing it. Routing-consistency, policy-violation and session-based attacks have no cryptographic answer at all; operators are left with control-plane policing and max-prefix limits.
https://blog.apnic.net/2026/08/26/beyond-origin-validation-four-classes-of-routing-attack-nobody-is-validating/
*APNIC · Aug 26*

**BGP Role model: tracking the adoption of RFC 9234**
Cloudflare measured RFC 9234 deployment by watching which peer ASes send the Only-to-Customer attribute, and found something operators need to know: two large Tier-1 networks strip OTC from routes they forward, quietly defeating leak prevention for anyone downstream who adopted it early. They're engaging with those networks; in the meantime, don't assume your OTC survives transit.
https://blog.cloudflare.com/rfc9234-bgp-role-model/
*blog.cloudflare.com · Aug 18*

**Multivendor SR-MPLS**
A three-vendor lab — Arista EOS, FRRouting, SR Linux — that works, and then the interop trap: each vendor ships a different default Segment Routing Global Block, so Arista expects label 900003 where FRR uses 24003 for the same destination. The recommendation is to pin the same SRGB everywhere. Reproducible via netlab, with the topology in a repo.
https://blog.ipspace.net/2026/08/sr-mpls-multivendor/
*ipSpace.net · Aug 25*

**Arista cEOS does not apply ACLs to control-plane traffic**
A specific containerlab finding with a broader moral aimed squarely at the digital-twin enthusiasts: the virtual image doesn't reproduce the platform behaviour you're testing for, so a passing test means less than it looks like.
https://blog.ipspace.net/2026/08/arista-ceos-acl-control-plane-traffic/
*ipSpace.net · Aug 24*

**Exploring the BGP neighbor CONNECT state**
Follow-up to an earlier post, prompted by a reader claiming Cisco IOS/XE behaves differently. Protocol-mechanics detail of the sort that only matters when a session won't come up and nobody can say why.
https://blog.ipspace.net/2026/08/exploring-bgp-connect-state/
*ipSpace.net · Aug 18*

**An interactive introduction to the spanning tree protocol**
Vincent Bernat builds STP up from the problem it solves, with interactive diagrams that make root election and port states legible. The best explanation of a protocol most people learned badly and have been guessing at since.
https://vincent.bernat.ch/en/blog/2026-spanning-tree
*vincent.bernat.ch · Aug 25*

**Chamelio: a chameleonic network stack that adapts to every tenant**
A provider-managed network stack for cloud VMs that runs in userspace on the host and lets tenants upload custom transport behaviour — an unusual answer to the tension between operator control and tenant flexibility.
https://blog.apnic.net/2026/08/14/chamelio-a-chameleonic-network-stack-that-adapts-to-every-tenant/
*APNIC · Aug 14*

**Cloudflare DDoS threat report, H1 2026**
A 519% surge in hyper-volumetric attacks in the first half of the year, with 1 Tbps events becoming routine and DNS floods driving much of the growth. Vendor-sourced measurement, so read the methodology, but the shape of the trend is the useful part.
https://blog.cloudflare.com/ddos-threat-report-2026-h1/
*blog.cloudflare.com · Aug 11*

**At carrier scale, DNS forwarding becomes a routing problem**
Why source IPs, return paths and BGP start dictating DNS behaviour once you're forwarding at carrier volume, and the case for separating the control plane from DNS processing. Single-practitioner account, but the failure modes described are specific.
https://medium.com/@iman.mir/at-carrier-scale-dns-forwarding-becomes-a-routing-problem-b2af21e6c15c
*Medium · Aug 21*

**A history of transit-free tier 1 networks**
How the transit-free club formed, who left it, and what the settlement-free peering economics looked like at each stage. Context for anyone whose peering strategy was inherited rather than designed.
https://anuragbhatia.com/post/2026/08/history-of-transit-free-networks/

## AI Deals

A thin fortnight. Three offers were worth the space; nothing else cleared the bar.

**GLM-5.3-Flash: MIT open weights, plus a launch discount to September 9**
Z.ai released a 320-billion-parameter mixture-of-experts model with 18B active, natively multimodal, with a 1,048,576-token context window — and put the weights on Hugging Face under MIT, which makes self-hosting the zero-cost path. On the API it's $0.15 per million input tokens and $0.50 output, halved to $0.075 and $0.25 under a launch promotion running through September 9. Cached input is $0.03 per million.
https://z.ai/blog/glm-5.3-flash
*z.ai · Aug 26*

**Proliferate — a self-hostable open-source AI IDE**
An AGPL-3.0 workspace for running Claude Code, Codex, OpenCode, Cursor and Grok side by side in isolated workspaces with shared tools. The full control plane is self-hostable, with deployment guides for Docker Compose, Kubernetes, AWS, GCP and Azure; you supply Postgres and your own model keys, so the software is free and the inference isn't.
https://github.com/proliferate-ai/proliferate
*GitHub · Show HN Aug 21 · AGPL-3.0*

**Shoehorn — quantize any model down to fit your machine**
An MIT-licensed tool for getting models onto hardware you already own rather than renting inference. Useful precisely when the free tier you were relying on tightens.
https://notactuallytreyanastasio.github.io/shoehorn/
*GitHub · Aug 13*
